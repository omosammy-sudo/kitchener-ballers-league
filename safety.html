Important Announcement

Player Safety

Injury prevention for every game

Following recent injuries during our community football games, we are introducing new safety measures to reduce the risk of injuries and better protect every player. The safety and well-being of everyone in our football community remain a top priority.

1. 🛡️ Health & Injury Insurance

All players must have the insurance cover described on our insurance page before taking part in games.

Insurance requirements →

2. 🏃 Mandatory Warm-Up & Stretching

Effective immediately, all players must take part in a 5–10 minute warm-up before games.

No warm-up, no play.

Warm-ups include light jogging, dynamic stretching, and mobility exercises to prepare players and reduce injury risk. Henry and Hooda will lead the fitness warm-up on gamedays.

3. ⚽ Mandatory Shin Guards

From December 20, 2026, all players must wear shin guards, long socks, and indoor cleats to take part in our community football games.

No shin guards, no play. This rule applies to everyone without exception.

4. 🩹 Community First Aid Kit

We will provide a fully equipped first aid kit for use during community games, so we can respond quickly to minor injuries. It is funded from community money, so keeping registration fees up to date matters. See fees →

5. 🚑 CPR & Basic First Aid Training

We will work towards having at least two designated community members trained in CPR and basic first aid at our games, so someone can help while waiting for professional responders.

We will also set up a basic emergency procedure, including when to call 911 and how to respond to serious injuries.

Our Commitment

Football brings us together, but everyone's health and safety must always come first.

We cannot eliminate every injury, but we can take practical steps to reduce risks, improve preparedness, and look out for one another. These measures are being introduced not to make football difficult for anyone, but to make sure everyone can enjoy the game in a safer and more responsible environment.

Let's play hard, play fair, and most importantly, play safe.

Community Football Management

In an Emergency

If someone is seriously hurt, call 911 first, then tell the referee or a committee member.
